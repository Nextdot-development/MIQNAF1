Mechanism of Action

Nafithromycin binds the 50S subunit and stops the peptide chain moving from the A site to the P site

Translation stalled — the tRNA is held at the A site and the peptide chain stops growing.
  1. 01

    Binds to the 50S ribosomal subunit

    Nafithromycin, a lactone-ketolide, binds the 50S subunit at the entrance of the nascent peptide exit tunnel, anchoring to domain V of the 23S rRNA with an additional ketolide-type contact in domain II that increases binding affinity.

  2. 02

    Blocks translocation of the peptide chain

    With the tunnel entrance occupied, the peptidyl-tRNA cannot move from the A site to the P site, so the ribosome stalls and the mRNA is no longer read codon by codon.

  3. 03

    Inhibits polypeptide chain elongation

    Elongation halts after only a few amino acids; the incomplete peptidyl-tRNA dissociates from the ribosome, so no functional protein is made.

  4. 04

    Growth and multiplication are inhibited

    Without new proteins the bacterium can neither grow nor multiply. The effect is bacteriostatic for most organisms and bactericidal against several key respiratory pathogens.
