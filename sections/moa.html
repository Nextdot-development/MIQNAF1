Dual Ribosomal Binding

Mechanism of Action

Nafithromycin anchors at two ribosomal sites at once — so neither erm(B) methylation nor mef(A) efflux can switch it off.

  1. 01

    Binds to the 50S ribosomal subunit

    Nafithromycin, a lactone ketolide, binds to the 50S subunit at the entrance of the nascent peptide exit tunnel, anchoring to domain V of the 23S rRNA with an additional ketolide-type contact in domain II that increases binding affinity.

  2. 02

    Blocks translocation of the peptide chain

    With the tunnel entrance occupied, the peptidyl-tRNA cannot move from the A site to the P site, so the ribosome stalls and the mRNA is no longer read codon by codon.

  3. 03

    Inhibits polypeptide chain elongation

    Elongation halts after only a few amino acids; the incomplete peptidyl-tRNA dissociates from the ribosome, so no functional protein is made.

  4. 04

    Growth and multiplication are inhibited

    Without new proteins the bacterium can neither grow nor multiply. The effect is bacteriostatic for most organisms and bactericidal against several key respiratory pathogens.

  1. 01

    Exit tunnel is open

    With no drug bound to domain V of the 23S rRNA, the growing peptide threads freely out through the 50S exit tunnel.

  2. 02

    Translocation proceeds

    EF-G–driven translocation shifts the peptidyl-tRNA from the A site to the P site, the deacylated tRNA leaves via the E site, and the mRNA advances by one codon.

  3. 03

    Chain elongation continues

    Each elongation cycle adds one amino acid, and the polypeptide lengthens as it passes through the exit tunnel.

  4. 04

    Bacterium grows and multiplies

    Complete, functional proteins are produced, supporting bacterial growth and multiplication.
